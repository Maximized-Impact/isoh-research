Why Phone Calls Need a Neurodivergent-First Redesign

What the paper argues

The phone call is the only major communication technology whose basic structure has never been redesigned. Email gained threading and scheduling, messaging gained read receipts, video calls gained captions. The voice call still works the way it did in 1876: it starts without agreement, runs without visible time, and ends only when someone finds the courage to end it.

For tens of millions of people that design is a barrier, not an inconvenience. People with ADHD lose track of time during calls and come to avoid them. Many autistic adults rank the phone call as their least preferred way to communicate. For people managing chronic illness, and the caregivers who support them, calls carry a heavy coordination burden, and both live with the fear of a call that does not get through.

The paper proposes Health Communications Technology (HCT): technology that changes the structure of the call itself (its timing, sensory environment, consent and emergency behaviour) rather than delivering health information, which is the job of health records, health apps and telemedicine. It presents Yapper Phone, an Android dialer publicly available on Google Play, as a case study, states plainly that no clinical trials have yet been conducted, and proposes six studies for independent validation.

Features designed for the people who struggle most with phone calls, such as visible timers, agreed durations and guaranteed emergency reach, turn out to be features almost everyone wants. That is the paper's central claim, and it is offered as a testable proposition, not a settled fact.

The five design principles of HCT

Time Intelligence
Elapsed and remaining time are made visible and negotiable during communication.
Sensory Modification
The user shapes the call's auditory environment; sound is a configured choice, never a universal default.
Consent Architecture
Both parties agree on the call's length and purpose before it begins.
Emergency Priority
Calls that must get through do get through, past Do Not Disturb and silent mode, and a lock-screen medical card is readable without unlocking the phone.
Progressive Disclosure
Complexity is shown only to those who want it.

The app as a research instrument

The app itself is offered to researchers as a research instrument: its time signals and focus sounds work inside real calls and also system-wide, behind any other media, all day, and it measures, on the user's own phone, adherence to agreed call lengths and overtime.

Free for study participants. Yapper Phone is provided at no cost to participants in any of the six proposed studies.

Academic partners are actively sought. The studies are offered to any university or research group.

How independence is kept

  • Studies of what the app's features do are designed, run and analysed by independent researchers.
  • The Institute sees no participant data before publication.
  • No veto and no delay over results. Review before publication is limited to product facts and confidential intellectual property, within 30 days.
  • Authorship follows the ICMJE criteria. Providing the instrument alone earns an acknowledgement, not authorship.
  • After publication, de-identified data are shared openly or under a data-sharing agreement, and any secondary analysis by the Institute is labelled as such.
  • The Institute is a research programme of a registered association with no owners, which under Finnish law cannot be sold or converted into a for-profit company. The research agenda cannot be bought.
